\begin{abstract}
We give an exact arithmetic and representation-theoretic description of a finite Weil
filtration generated by three-character Heisenberg fingerprints.
For every odd prime $q$ and non-zero total character $c=c_1+c_2+c_3$, the cumulative Fourier
mode set at depth $n$ is the cyclic sumset
\[
  K_n=c[-(n+1),n+1]+\{0,\pm(c_2+c_3)\}+\{0,\pm c_3\}.
\]
This closed form turns the filtration into a union of at most nine cyclic intervals.
It yields an exact spacing criterion for boundary porosity and reduces the full Weil
stabiliser to the multiplicative symmetry group of $K_n$.
Weyl quantisation then identifies the induced Mackey carrier internally as a direct summand
of $\End(\C^q)$.
Its generic principal-series constituents occur in canonical multiplicity-two pairs with
commutant $M_2(\C)$.
The finite Harper operator selects one axis in every such doublet and produces the universal
normalised split $1\pm q^{-1/2}$.
The eliminated-sector residue supplies a second axis precisely when a short boundary
character sum over at most four hole pairs is non-zero: a Galois separation theorem
eliminates the cyclotomic phase determinant for every generic character, and the vanishing
locus of the boundary sum is completely classified into antipodal-pair, cube-triple, and
double-pair mechanisms.
The odd Weyl-symbol sector is itself a pure doublet reservoir for $q\equiv1\pmod4$, but it is
exactly protected by the parity-even algebra generated by the deposited projectors and
Harper dynamics.
An orbit-divisibility sieve constrains every multiplicative stabiliser, and an
unconditional almost-periodicity lemma, a run-correspondence argument, and a Diophantine
descent prove that the stabiliser is $\{\pm1\}$ whenever every run and gap of the mode set
is longer than the run count.
Completeness of the exceptional orders is thereby reduced to a degenerate boundary regime,
where a fourth-moment argument proves that every proper Gaussian box has exact stabiliser
$C_4$, and a free four-point complement orbit has the same exact stabiliser.
An exhaustive audit through $q=151$ finds $75$ exceptional sets, all of order four or six
and all within three refined mechanisms, with no order eight at $q=193$, $241$, $257$.
Finally, the porous depths of a uniform block converge to an explicit point process, with
count law $(3/20,7/24,9/40,5/24,1/8)$, mean $28/15$, and piecewise affine intensity.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretive status.}
The construction supplies finite two-level spectral splitting, mixing frames, and
mass-square kinematics at an operator stratum.
It does not derive Dirac masses, an absolute mass scale, Standard-Model generations,
hypercharge, or a matter-sector Higgs mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem, Results, and Epistemic Scope}
\label{sec:introduction}

The finite Weil representation is simultaneously a model of exact harmonic analysis over
finite fields and the carrier used by the O12 spectral-admissibility
computation~\cite{Weil1964,Gerardin1977,Beau2026a16}.
The present paper isolates a mathematical structure that was hidden by the original
Gram--Schmidt implementation: the cumulative O12 filtration is a Fourier coordinate
filtration whose mode sets have a closed additive form.

Four questions then become finite and exact.
First, which subgroup of $\G$ stabilises a proper filtration space?
Second, where can multiplicity-two factors occur once the irreducible parity sectors of the
finite Weil module itself have scalar commutant?
Third, which deposited operators select axes inside those factors?
Fourth, how frequent is boundary porosity: what is the asymptotic distribution of the
porous depths of a uniform admissible block?

The answers form the following chain.
The mode space has a complete stabiliser controlled by a multiplicative set stabiliser.
That set stabiliser is rigid: an additive almost-periodicity argument proves it equal to
$\{\pm1\}$ outside a degenerate boundary regime.
The degenerate regime itself hosts an infinite uniform family of Gaussian boxes together
with two further refined mechanisms, exactly audited through $q=151$.
Every proper Gaussian box and every free four-point complement orbit has exact stabiliser
$C_4$.
The porosity statistics have an exact scaling limit: the porous depths of a uniform block
converge to an explicit point process with rational count law and piecewise affine
intensity.
The Weyl-symbol algebra embeds the relevant induced representation into
$\End(\C^q)$.
Mackey induction and the Weyl pairing of principal series create canonical $M_2(\C)$
commutants at this operator stratum~\cite{Mackey1952,Iwahori1964}.
Harper dynamics gives a first axis, while boundary porosity can give a second.
The second axis is governed by a single short character sum: a Galois separation argument
makes the cyclotomic phase determinant redundant, and the vanishing of that sum is
completely classified on at most four hole pairs through the theory of vanishing sums of
roots of unity~\cite{LamLeung2000,ConwayJones1976}.
Parity simultaneously protects the complementary odd sector.

The paper separates four epistemic levels.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The sumset formula, spacing theorem, stabiliser formula, additive rigidity theorem,
  exact Gaussian-box and free-complement stabilisers, Weyl-symbol decomposition, and
  parity closure are proved uniformly.
  \item The Hecke decompositions are standard finite-group consequences, with conventions
  stated explicitly.
  \item Exact cyclotomic and rational-polyhedral computations are machine-certified;
  their claims are asserted either uniformly from their formulas or for the cases actually
  certified.
  \item Multi-prime frequencies and physical readings are labelled computational or
  interpretive and are not promoted to theorems.
\end{enumerate}

No identification with a Standard-Model representation is made.
In particular, a multiplicity doublet in an operator algebra is not by itself a weak-isospin
doublet of fermionic matter.

\section{Finite Heisenberg and Weil Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $q$ be an odd prime and write elements of $\Heis$ as $(a,b,z)$ with product
\begin{equation}
  (a,b,z)(a',b',z')=(a+a',b+b',z+z'+ab').
  \label{eq:heisenberg-product}
\end{equation}
Let $X=(1,0,0)$ and $Y=(0,1,0)$, and use the symmetric Cayley generating set
$\{X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}\}$.

Fix a non-zero central character $c\in\Fq^*$ and set
$\psi_c(t)=\exp(2\pi i ct/q)$.
The Schr\"odinger representation on $V_c=\C^{\Fq}$ is taken in the convention
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\rho_c(a,b,z)f\bigr)(x)=\psi_c\!\bigl(z+b(x-a)\bigr)f(x-a).
  \label{eq:schrodinger}
\end{equation}
Its extension to $\G$ is the finite Weil representation.
Only conjugation identities are used below, so the scalar metaplectic normalisation does not
affect the statements.

For $v=(a,b)\in\Fq^2$, define the symmetrically ordered displacement
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(W_c(a,b)f\bigr)(x)
  =\psi_c\!\left(b\left(x-\frac{a}{2}\right)\right)f(x-a).
  \label{eq:weyl-displacement}
\end{equation}
This is the finite-field analogue of Weyl's symmetric quantisation
prescription~\cite{Weyl1927}.
These $q^2$ operators are an orthogonal basis of $\End(V_c)$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \rho_c(g)W_c(v)\rho_c(g)^{-1}=W_c\bigl(\varphi(g)v\bigr),
  \qquad g\in\G.
  \label{eq:weyl-covariance}
\end{equation}
In the coordinates of~\eqref{eq:weyl-displacement}, the linear phase-space action is
\begin{equation}
  \varphi(g)=R_0g^{-\mathsf{T}}R_0,
  \qquad R_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
  \label{eq:phase-space-action}
\end{equation}

For a triple $(c_1,c_2,c_3)\in(\Fq^*)^3$, the O12 fingerprint vectors are products of
three Schr\"odinger vectors and hence single Fourier characters.
We assume throughout that
\begin{equation}
  c:=c_1+c_2+c_3\ne0.
  \label{eq:total-character}
\end{equation}
The cumulative filtration space is therefore
\begin{equation}
  W_{<n}=\Span\{\chi_k:k\in K_n\},
  \qquad \chi_k(x)=q^{-1/2}\psi_1(kx),
  \label{eq:fourier-filtration}
\end{equation}
for a finite set $K_n\subset\Fq$.

\section{Closed Sumset Form and the Spacing Theorem}
\label{sec:sumset}

Set
\begin{equation}
  u=c_2+c_3,
  \qquad v=c_3.
  \label{eq:u-v}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Closed mode-set formula]
  \label{thm:closed-mode-set}
  At cumulative BFS depth $n\geq0$,
  \begin{equation}
    K_n=c[-(n+1),n+1]+\{0,\pm u\}+\{0,\pm v\}
    \quad\text{in }\Fq.
    \label{eq:closed-mode-set}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, $K_n=-K_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The second Heisenberg coordinate is additive:
  $b(gs)=b(g)+b(s)$.
  On the symmetric generating set, $b(s)$ belongs to $\{0,\pm1\}$.
  The BFS ball of depth $n$ realises every integer $b(g)\in[-n,n]$, because powers of
  $Y$ realise the endpoints and intermediate values.
  Conversely, each generator changes the second coordinate by at most one, so every ball
  element satisfies $|b(g)|\leq n$.

  Write $t_i=b(s_i)$ for the three post-shell generator choices.
  The three successive second coordinates are
  \[
    b_1=b(g)+t_1,
    \quad b_2=b(g)+t_1+t_2,
    \quad b_3=b(g)+t_1+t_2+t_3.
  \]
  Hence the fingerprint frequency is
  \[
    c_1b_1+c_2b_2+c_3b_3
    =c\bigl(b(g)+t_1\bigr)+ut_2+vt_3.
  \]
  As $b(g)$ and $t_1$ vary, $b(g)+t_1$ fills $[-(n+1),n+1]$.
  The variables $t_2,t_3$ independently fill $\{0,\pm1\}$.
  This proves~\eqref{eq:closed-mode-set}.
  Each summand is centrally symmetric, proving $K_n=-K_n$.
\end{proof}

Rescale by $c^{-1}$ and define
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde u=u/c,
  \quad \widetilde v=v/c,
  \quad
  S=\{a\widetilde u+b\widetilde v:a,b\in\{-1,0,1\}\}.
  \label{eq:centre-set}
\end{equation}
The rescaled set $c^{-1}K_n$ is a union of cyclic intervals of radius $n+1$ centred on the
at most nine distinct points of $S$.

Order the points of $S$ cyclically and denote their positive cyclic spacings by
$\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_{|S|}$.
Define the rescaled flanked-hole set
\begin{equation}
  H(K_n)=\{h\notin c^{-1}K_n:h-1,h+1\in c^{-1}K_n\}.
  \label{eq:flanked-holes-rescaled}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Spacing-parity criterion]
  \label{thm:spacing}
  Before saturation,
  \begin{equation}
    H(K_n)\ne\varnothing
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    2n+4\in\{\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_{|S|}\}.
    \label{eq:spacing-criterion}
  \end{equation}
  Consequently an odd spacing never produces a flanked hole.
  An even spacing $\delta$ produces one at the unique depth
  $n=(\delta-4)/2$, provided that depth is pre-saturation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Consecutive windows of radius $n+1$ whose centres have cyclic distance $\delta_i$ leave
  exactly $\delta_i-2(n+1)-1=\delta_i-(2n+3)$ uncovered sites.
  A flanked hole is precisely a complementary component of cardinality one.
  This gives $\delta_i-(2n+3)=1$, which is~\eqref{eq:spacing-criterion}.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete Weil Stabiliser}
\label{sec:stabiliser}

For a non-empty proper set $K\subset\Fq$, define
\begin{equation}
  A_K=\{a\in\Fq^*:aK=K\}.
  \label{eq:AK}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Complete stabiliser]
  \label{thm:stabiliser}
  The stabiliser in the finite Weil action of the Fourier coordinate space
  $W_K=\Span\{\chi_k:k\in K\}$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \Stab_{\G}(W_K)
    =\left\{
      \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\t&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
      :t\in\Fq,\ a\in A_K
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:complete-stabiliser}
  \end{equation}
  It has order $q|A_K|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  In the Fourier basis, the lower unipotent radical acts diagonally and the diagonal torus
  sends the label $k$ to a non-zero scalar multiple of $k$.
  Hence a lower-triangular element preserves $W_K$ exactly when its multiplier lies in
  $A_K$.

  If $g$ is outside the lower Borel, its Weil kernel is a non-degenerate quadratic Gauss
  kernel.
  Every matrix coefficient of the image of a Fourier basis vector is then non-zero.
  Thus the image has full Fourier support and cannot lie in a non-empty proper coordinate
  subspace.
  The two statements give~\eqref{eq:complete-stabiliser}.
\end{proof}

Because Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set} gives $K_n=-K_n$, one always has
$\{\pm1\}\subseteq A_{K_n}$.
For the eight deposited cases, $A_{K_n}=\{\pm1\}$ and the stabiliser is the abelian group
$\langle-I\rangle U_-$ of order $2q$.

This equality is generic in the computational audit but not universal.
For example, an exact depth-zero case at $q=29$ has $A_K\cong C_4$, while one at $q=31$
has $A_K\cong C_6$.
The general theorem is therefore~\eqref{eq:complete-stabiliser}, not the special value
$A_K=\{\pm1\}$.

The exceptional groups nevertheless obey a sharp elementary sieve.

\begin{proposition}[Orbit-divisibility sieve]
  \label{prop:AK-divisibility}
  For every proper $K_n$, the group $A_{K_n}$ is cyclic of even order and
  \begin{equation}
    |A_{K_n}|\mid\gcd(q-1,|K_n|-1).
    \label{eq:AK-divisibility}
  \end{equation}
  Equivalently, $|A_{K_n}|$ divides both $|K_n|-1$ and $q-|K_n|$.
  In particular, if the greatest common divisor in~\eqref{eq:AK-divisibility} is two,
  then $A_{K_n}=\{\pm1\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The multiplicative group $\Fq^*$ is cyclic, so every subgroup is cyclic.
  Central symmetry gives $-1\in A_{K_n}$, hence the order is even.
  The origin belongs to $K_n$ and is fixed, while the action of $A_{K_n}$ on
  $K_n\setminus\{0\}$ is free.
  Thus $|A_{K_n}|$ divides $|K_n|-1$.
  The same free action on the complement gives divisibility of $q-|K_n|$.
\end{proof}

There are two uniform exceptional families.
They explain the orders four and six without asserting that these are the only possible
orders for the full equal-radius nine-centre class.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform $C_4$ and $C_6$ families]
  \label{prop:exceptional-families}
  Suppose first that $a^2=-1$ in $\Fq$ and $q>5$.
  At depth zero, the normalised parameters $\widetilde u=0$ and
  $\widetilde v=a$ give a proper set with $A_K\cong C_4$.

  Suppose instead that $a^2+a+1=0$, $a\ne1$, and $q>19$.
  At depth zero, the parameters $\widetilde u=a$ and
  $\widetilde v=a+1$ give a proper set with $A_K\cong C_6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  In the first family,
  \[
    K=\{m+na:m,n\in\{-1,0,1\}\}.
  \]
  Multiplication by $a$ sends $(m,n)$ to $(-n,m)$, so $C_4\subseteq A_K$.
  The nine elements are distinct for $q>5$.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:AK-divisibility} leaves only orders four and eight.
  If the order were eight, then $K\setminus\{0\}$ would be the order-eight subgroup and
  would contain $1+a$.
  But $(1+a)^8=16$, so this would require $q\mid15$, contrary to $q>5$.

  In the second family, write an element as $m+na$.
  The set $K$ consists of the origin and three six-element orbits under
  \begin{equation}
    (a+1)(m+na)=(m-n)+ma.
    \label{eq:C6-action}
  \end{equation}
  The nineteen elements are distinct for $q>19$: the norm of every non-zero difference
  is one of $1,3,4,7,9,12,13,16$.
  Hence $C_6\subseteq A_K$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:AK-divisibility} leaves only orders
  six and eighteen.
  Order eighteen would make $K\setminus\{0\}$ a multiplicative subgroup.
  Since $2\in K$, it would force $4\in K$.
  Substitution in the nineteen forms reduces this possibility to prime divisors of
  $7,12,13,19,21,28,31$.
  Together with the cubic-root condition, this leaves
  $q\in\{7,13,19,31\}$; the first three cases are saturated.
  At $q=31$, closure would further force $8\in K$, while direct substitution gives
  possible divisors only from $43,52,57,67,73,84,91$, so $8\notin K$.
  Thus the proper stabiliser has order six.
\end{proof}

Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} proves that such exceptions are confined to a degenerate boundary
regime, and adds a third uniform family at the near-saturation end of that regime.

\section{Additive Rigidity of the Multiplicative Stabiliser}
\label{sec:rigidity}

Throughout this section $K\subset\Fq$ is proper and non-empty, with maximal cyclic intervals
(\emph{runs}) $I_1,\ldots,I_m$ and complementary maximal intervals (\emph{gaps})
$G_1,\ldots,G_m$, listed in cyclic order.
Write $I_i=[x_i,y_i]$ and $L_i=|I_i|$.
For the mode sets of Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set} one has $m\leq9$.

\begin{lemma}[Unconditional almost-periodicity]
  \label{lem:almost-period}
  For every $a\in A_K$,
  \begin{equation}
    |K\cap(K+a)|=|K|-m,
    \qquad
    |K\,\triangle\,(K+a)|=2m.
    \label{eq:almost-period}
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let $C(d)=|K\cap(K-d)|$.
  For $a\in A_K$ the bijection $x\mapsto a^{-1}x$ of $\Fq$ maps $K\cap(K-ad)$ onto
  $K\cap(K-d)$, so $C(ad)=C(d)$ for every $d$.
  Each run of length $L_i$ contains exactly $L_i-1$ elements $x$ with $x+1\in K$, so
  $C(1)=|K|-m$.
  Taking $d=1$ gives the first equality, and the second follows from $|K+a|=|K|$.
\end{proof}

Every multiplicative stabiliser of a nine-centre mode set is therefore an additive
almost-period of $K$ with symmetric-difference defect at most eighteen.
The multiplicative question becomes a question about the additive boundary of $K$.

\begin{lemma}[Run correspondence]
  \label{lem:run-correspondence}
  Assume every run and every gap of $K$ has length at least $m+1$.
  Then for every $a\in A_K$ there are a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and
  non-negative integers $\lambda_i,\rho_i,\alpha_i,\beta_i$ with
  $\sum_i(\lambda_i+\rho_i)=\sum_i(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m$, such that each
  $M_i=[x_i+a+\lambda_i,\,y_i+a-\rho_i]$ is non-empty, satisfies
  $M_i=(I_i+a)\cap K\subseteq I_{\sigma(i)}$, and starts at $x_{\sigma(i)}+\alpha_i$.
  Consequently
  \begin{equation}
    ma\equiv t\pmod q,
    \qquad
    t=\sum_i\alpha_i-\sum_i\lambda_i,
    \qquad
    0<|t|\leq m.
    \label{eq:residue-bound}
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Translation by $a$ preserves the run--gap pattern, so the sets $J_i=I_i+a$ are the runs of
  $K+a$, separated by gaps of the same lengths as those of $K$.
  Let $U_i=J_i\setminus K$; Lemma~\ref{lem:almost-period} gives
  $\sum_i|U_i|=|(K+a)\setminus K|=m$.

  A gap $G$ of $K$ meeting $J_i$ either contains an endpoint of $J_i$, or satisfies
  $G\subseteq J_i$ or $J_i\subseteq G$.
  The last two options put at least $\min(|G|,|J_i|)\geq m+1>m$ points into $U_i$, which is
  impossible.
  Distinct gaps are disjoint, so at most one gap contains each endpoint of $J_i$.
  Hence $U_i$ is the union of a prefix of length $\lambda_i\geq0$ and a suffix of length
  $\rho_i\geq0$ of $J_i$, and $M_i=J_i\setminus U_i=J_i\cap K$ is an interval of length
  $L_i-\lambda_i-\rho_i\geq L_i-m\geq1$.
  Being a non-empty interval inside $K$, $M_i$ lies in a unique run $I_{\sigma(i)}$.

  Suppose $\sigma(i)=\sigma(j)=k$ with $i\ne j$, and choose $M_i$ and $M_j$ adjacent among
  the middles contained in $I_k$, with $M_j$ following $M_i$ inside $I_k$.
  If $\rho_i>0$, the point following $M_i$ lies in $U_i$, hence outside $K$; then $M_i$ ends
  at the right endpoint of $I_k$ and no point of $I_k$ lies beyond it, contradicting
  $M_j\subseteq I_k$.
  So $\rho_i=0$ and $M_i$ ends where $J_i$ ends.
  The gap of $K+a$ that follows $J_i$ has length at least $m+1$ and lies between two points
  of the interval $I_k$, hence inside $I_k\subseteq K$.
  Its points are outside $K+a$, so $|K\setminus(K+a)|\geq m+1$, contradicting
  Lemma~\ref{lem:almost-period}.
  Hence $\sigma$ is injective, therefore a permutation.

  If $p\in I_k\cap(K+a)$, then $p\in J_l\cap K=M_l\subseteq I_{\sigma(l)}$ for some $l$, so
  $l=\sigma^{-1}(k)$: every run satisfies $I_k\cap(K+a)=M_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}$.
  Writing $\alpha_i\geq0$ and $\beta_i\geq0$ for the numbers of points of $I_{\sigma(i)}$
  before and after $M_i$, the identity $|K\setminus(K+a)|=m$ gives
  $\sum_i(\alpha_i+\beta_i)=m$.

  The left endpoint of $M_i$ equals both $x_i+a+\lambda_i$ and $x_{\sigma(i)}+\alpha_i$.
  Summing over $i$ and using $\sum_ix_{\sigma(i)}=\sum_ix_i$ gives
  $ma\equiv\sum_i(\alpha_i-\lambda_i)\pmod q$.
  The bound $|t|\leq m$ follows from $0\leq\sum_i\alpha_i\leq m$ and
  $0\leq\sum_i\lambda_i\leq m$.
  Finally $t\ne0$: the hypotheses force $q\geq2m(m+1)>m$, so $ma\ne0$ in $\Fq$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular-regime rigidity]
  \label{thm:rigidity}
  If every run and every gap of $K$ has length at least $m+1$, then
  $A_K\subseteq\{\pm1\}$.
  For the centrally symmetric mode sets of Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set} this gives
  $A_K=\{\pm1\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The hypotheses force $q\geq2m(m+1)>2m^2$, so the Diophantine threshold is automatic.
  Fix $a\in A_K$ of order $h$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:run-correspondence} to each power
  $a^j\in A_K$: there are integers $t_j$ with $ma^j\equiv t_j\pmod q$ and
  $0<|t_j|\leq m$.
  Multiplying two such congruences gives
  $t_it_j\equiv m^2a^{i+j}\equiv mt_{i+j}\pmod q$; both sides lie in
  $[-m^2,m^2]\subset(-q/2,q/2)$, so $t_it_j=mt_{i+j}$ holds in $\mathbb{Z}$.
  By induction $t_j=t_1^j/m^{j-1}$ for all $j\geq1$.
  Since $a^h=1$, the residue $t_h\equiv m\pmod q$ with $|t_h|\leq m$ forces $t_h=m$, so
  $(t_1/m)^h=1$.
  The only rational roots of unity are $\pm1$, hence $t_1=\pm m$ and
  $a\equiv m^{-1}t_1=\pm1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reduction to the degenerate regime]
  \label{cor:degenerate-reduction}
  Every proper mode set with $A_K\ne\{\pm1\}$ has a run or a gap of length at most
  $m\leq9$.
  The completeness question for the exceptional orders is thereby reduced to this degenerate
  boundary regime.
\end{corollary}

All known exceptional families indeed live there.
The depth-zero $C_4$ and $C_6$ families of Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families} have
runs of length three.
At the opposite, near-saturation end of the regime sits a third uniform family with a
four-point complement.

\begin{proposition}[Near-saturation $C_4$ family]
  \label{prop:near-saturation}
  Let $K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{w,w+1,-w-1,-w\}$ with the four elements distinct and non-zero.
  Then $A_K\ne\{\pm1\}$ if and only if
  \begin{equation}
    2w^2+2w+1\equiv0\pmod q,
    \label{eq:near-saturation}
  \end{equation}
  in which case $A_K=\langle(w+1)/w\rangle\cong C_4$ and $K^{\mathrm{c}}$ is a single free
  $A_K$-orbit.
  Equation~\eqref{eq:near-saturation} is solvable exactly when $q\equiv1\pmod4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  One has $A_K=A_{K^{\mathrm{c}}}$, and $0\in K$ makes the action of $A_K$ on
  $K^{\mathrm{c}}$ free: $az=z$ with $z\ne0$ forces $a=1$.
  Hence $|A_K|$ divides four.
  If $|A_K|=4$, its generator $a$ spans the unique order-four subgroup of $\Fq^*$, so
  $a^2=-1$, and the orbit of $w$ is all of $K^{\mathrm{c}}$.
  From $aw\ne-w$ (else $a=-1$) we get $aw=\pm(w+1)$, hence $a=\pm(w+1)/w$, and $a^2=-1$ is
  equivalent to $(w+1)^2\equiv-w^2$, which is~\eqref{eq:near-saturation}.
  Conversely, if $w$ solves~\eqref{eq:near-saturation}, set $a=(w+1)/w$.
  Then $a^2=-1$ and
  $aw=w+1$, $a(w+1)=a^2w=-w$, $a(-w)=-w-1$, $a(-w-1)=w$,
  so $a\in A_{K^{\mathrm{c}}}=A_K$, and the divisibility bound gives $A_K\cong C_4$.
  Solvability: the discriminant of $2w^2+2w+1$ is $-4$, a square modulo $q$ exactly when
  $q\equiv1\pmod4$.
\end{proof}

The family is realised by deposited-class mode sets.
At $q=73$, the normalised block $(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)=(46,3)$ reaches, at depth
$n=8$, the mode set with $K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{13,14,59,60\}$ and $w=13$; analogous
realisations occur at $q=109$ (depth $11$) and $q=173$ (depth $10$).
The sieve of Proposition~\ref{prop:AK-divisibility} is consistent: $|K|=q-4$ gives
$\gcd(q-1,|K|-1)=\gcd(q-1,4)$.

\begin{proposition}[Small complements]
  \label{prop:small-complement}
  If $|K^{\mathrm{c}}|=4$, then $|A_K|$ divides four; if $A_K=\langle a\rangle$ has order
  four, then $a^2=-1$ and $K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{z,az,-z,-az\}$ for any
  $z\in K^{\mathrm{c}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The action of $A_K$ on $K^{\mathrm{c}}$ is free as in
  Proposition~\ref{prop:near-saturation}, so $|A_K|$ divides $|K^{\mathrm{c}}|=4$.
  In the order-four case any point orbit has size four, hence equals $K^{\mathrm{c}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact free-orbit complement stabiliser]
  \label{cor:free-orbit-complement}
  Let $q\equiv1\pmod4$, let $i^2=-1$, and suppose
  \[
    K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{z,iz,-z,-iz\}
  \]
  with four distinct non-zero elements.
  Then $A_K=\langle i\rangle\cong C_4$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The subgroup $\langle i\rangle$ preserves $K^{\mathrm{c}}$, hence lies in $A_K$.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:small-complement} gives $|A_K|\mid4$, so equality follows.
\end{proof}

The free orbit of Proposition~\ref{prop:small-complement} is strictly broader than the
adjacent form of Proposition~\ref{prop:near-saturation}.
At $q=37$, the normalised block $(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)=(9,15)$ reaches, at depth
$n=1$, the mode set with $K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{3,18,19,34\}$: one free orbit of the order-four
multiplier $6$, whose centrally symmetric gap profile is one adjacent pair together with
two singleton gaps, not two opposite adjacent pairs.
At $q=41$, the audited orbit $\{3,14,27,38\}$ even has four singleton gaps.
A previous version of this paper stated that all audited four-point complements realise
the adjacent form; that sentence was false already within the earlier audited range and is
withdrawn.
All three centrally symmetric gap profiles of a four-point complement occur in the audited
range, and the sumset geometry excludes none of them.
Section~\ref{sec:degenerate} completes the refined mechanism inventory of the degenerate
regime.

\section{The Degenerate Regime: Gaussian Boxes and Refined Mechanisms}
\label{sec:degenerate}

Corollary~\ref{cor:degenerate-reduction} confines every exceptional stabiliser to mode sets
with a run or a gap of length at most the run count $m\leq9$.
This section refines the mechanism inventory of that regime.
Its first result shows that the depth-zero $C_4$ family of
Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families} is the smallest member of an infinite uniform
family, so the three family statements of the previous sections must not be read as an
exhaustive mechanism list.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian-box $C_4$ mechanism]
  \label{prop:gaussian-boxes}
  Let $q\equiv1\pmod4$, let $i\in\Fq$ satisfy $i^2=-1$, and let $r\geq1$ with $q>8r^2$.
  Define the Gaussian box
  \begin{equation}
    Q_r(i)=\{x+iy:x,y\in\mathbb{Z},\ |x|\leq r,\ |y|\leq r\}\subset\Fq.
    \label{eq:gaussian-box}
  \end{equation}
  Then $Q_r(i)$ has exactly $(2r+1)^2$ elements, is proper, and satisfies
  $\langle i\rangle\cong C_4\subseteq A_{Q_r(i)}$.
  For $1\leq r\leq4$ the box is a mode set of the equal-radius nine-centre class at depth
  $n=r-1$: the normalised parameters
  \begin{equation}
    (\widetilde u,\widetilde v)=(0,i),\ (i,-i),\ (2i,i),\ (3i,i)
    \qquad(r=1,2,3,4)
    \label{eq:box-parameters}
  \end{equation}
  are admissible and give $K_{r-1}=Q_r(i)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  If $x+iy=x'+iy'$ with $(x,y)\ne(x',y')$, then $(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2\equiv0\pmod q$ with
  $0<(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2\leq8r^2<q$, which is impossible; hence the $(2r+1)^2$ elements are
  distinct.
  For $r\geq2$ one has $(2r+1)^2\leq8r^2<q$, and for $r=1$ the congruence $q\equiv1\pmod4$
  with $q>8$ forces $q\geq13>9$; in both cases the box is proper.
  Multiplication by $i$ sends $x+iy$ to $-y+ix$ and preserves the box, so
  $\langle i\rangle\subseteq A_{Q_r(i)}$, and $i$ has order four.

  For the realisation, the centre sets~\eqref{eq:centre-set} of the four parameter pairs
  in~\eqref{eq:box-parameters} are
  $\{bi\}$, $\{(a-b)i\}$, $\{(2a+b)i\}$, and $\{(3a+b)i\}$ with $a,b\in\{-1,0,1\}$, and each
  equals $i\,[-r,r]$ for its radius $r$.
  The depth-$(r-1)$ windows have radius $r$, so
  \[
    K_{r-1}=\bigcup_{|k|\leq r}\{ik+j:|j|\leq r\}=Q_r(i).
  \]
  Admissibility requires $\widetilde v\ne0$, $\widetilde u\ne\widetilde v$, and
  $\widetilde u\ne1$.
  The first two conditions hold by inspection.
  For the third, $\widetilde u\in\{0,i,2i,3i\}$, and $\widetilde u=1$ would force
  $-1=1$, $-4=1$, or $-9=1$, hence $q\mid2$, $q\mid5$, or $q\mid10$, all excluded by
  $q>8r^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact Gaussian collision dichotomy]
  \label{lem:gaussian-collision-dichotomy}
  Let $q\equiv1\pmod4$, choose $0<u<v$ with $q=u^2+v^2$, and choose the root
  $i^2=-1$ for which $\pi=u+iv$ vanishes in $\Fq$.
  Let
  \[
    \Lambda=\pi\mathbb{Z}[i]
    =\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^2:x+iy=0\text{ in }\Fq\}.
  \]
  Every non-zero vector of $\Lambda$ has $\ell^\infty$-norm either $v$, attained by the
  four unit rotations of $\pi$, or at least $u+v$.
  Moreover, the reduction $[-r,r]^2\to Q_r(i)$ is injective if and only if
  \[
    \mu_\infty(\Lambda)>2r,
    \qquad
    \mu_\infty(\Lambda)\coloneqq
    \min_{0\ne\lambda\in\Lambda}\|\lambda\|_\infty=v.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Write $\lambda=\pi\gamma$.
  If $N(\gamma)=1$, then $\gamma$ is a Gaussian unit and the four possible vectors have
  sup-norm $v$.
  If $N(\gamma)=2$, the associates of
  \[
    \pi(1+i)=(u-v)+i(u+v)
  \]
  have sup-norm $u+v$.
  This case must be computed directly: the Euclidean bound alone does not separate it.
  There is no Gaussian integer of norm three.
  If $N(\gamma)\geq4$, then
  \[
    \|\lambda\|_\infty
    \geq\frac{|\lambda|}{\sqrt2}
    \geq\sqrt{2q}
    \geq u+v,
  \]
  where the last inequality is $(u+v)^2\leq2(u^2+v^2)$.
  This proves the short-vector dichotomy and $\mu_\infty(\Lambda)=v$.

  Two points of $[-r,r]^2$ have the same reduction exactly when their difference is a
  non-zero vector of $\Lambda\cap[-2r,2r]^2$.
  This proves the injectivity criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact stabiliser of every proper Gaussian box]
  \label{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser}
  Let $q\equiv1\pmod4$, let $i^2=-1$ in $\Fq$, and let $r\geq1$ be such that
  $Q_r(i)\ne\Fq$.
  Then
  \[
    A_{Q_r(i)}=\langle i\rangle\cong C_4.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The box is unchanged when $i$ is replaced by $-i$, so choose the root convention of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-collision-dichotomy}.
  Put
  \[
    S_4(Q_r)=\sum_{z\in Q_r(i)}z^4.
  \]
  Every $a\in A_{Q_r(i)}$ permutes the box, hence
  \begin{equation}
    S_4(Q_r)=a^4S_4(Q_r).
    \label{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-invariance}
  \end{equation}
  It is therefore enough to prove $S_4(Q_r)\ne0$.

  Suppose first that $\mu_\infty(\Lambda)>2r$.
  Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-collision-dichotomy} makes the square reduction injective.
  Direct summation over $z=x+iy$, using the standard second- and fourth-power sums,
  gives
  \begin{equation}
    S_4(Q_r)
    =-\frac{2r(r+1)(2r+1)^2(2r^2+2r+1)}{15}.
    \label{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-injective}
  \end{equation}
  Properness implies $2r+1<q$, since otherwise $[-r,r]$ already covers $\Fq$.
  Thus the first four numerator factors do not vanish modulo $q$.
  If $q$ divided $2r^2+2r+1=r^2+(r+1)^2$, then one of
  $(r,r+1)$ and $(r,-r-1)$ would belong to $\Lambda$ and have sup-norm at most $2r$,
  contradicting the present hypothesis.
  The smallest relevant prime is $q=13$, so $15$ is invertible and
  \eqref{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-injective} is non-zero.

  Suppose now that $\mu_\infty(\Lambda)=v\leq2r$.
  Set
  \[
    R=\frac{u+v-1}{2},
    \qquad
    t=R-r.
  \]
  We first record the collision count for any integer $s$ satisfying
  $v\leq2s<u+v$.
  The short-vector dichotomy shows that the only possible collision differences inside
  $[-s,s]^2$ are the four unit rotations of $\pi$.
  Every reduction fibre has multiplicity at most two: two distinct partners would differ
  by a vector of sup-norm at least $u+v>2s$.
  Representatives of the unordered double fibres are the two rectangles
  \[
    D_1(s)=[-s+u,s]\times[-s+v,s],
    \qquad
    D_2(s)=[-s,s-v]\times[-s+u,s].
  \]
  Their images are disjoint, since an overlap would create a fibre with three
  representatives.
  Each rectangle has $(2s+1-u)(2s+1-v)$ points, so
  \begin{equation}
    |Q_s(i)|=(2s+1)^2-2(2s+1-u)(2s+1-v).
    \label{eq:gaussian-collision-count}
  \end{equation}
  The value $s=R$ satisfies $v\leq2R<u+v$, and the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:gaussian-collision-count} is then $q$.
  Thus $Q_R(i)=\Fq$.
  Properness gives $r<R$, hence $2r<u+v$ and $t\geq1$.
  The present wrapped hypothesis also gives $v\leq2r$, so the same count applies at
  $s=r$.
  Write
  \[
    C_1=D_1(r),
    \qquad
    C_2=D_2(r).
  \]
  Wrappedness gives
  \begin{equation}
    2t=u+v-1-2r\leq u-1.
    \label{eq:gaussian-wrapped-bound}
  \end{equation}

  Let $\phi(x,y)=x+iy$.
  Subtracting one representative of every double fibre yields
  \begin{equation}
    S_4(Q_r)
    =\sum_{[-r,r]^2}\phi^4
     -\sum_{C_1}\phi^4
     -\sum_{C_2}\phi^4.
    \label{eq:gaussian-collision-moment}
  \end{equation}
  Applying the power-sum formulas to the three rectangles and substituting
  $r=(u+v-1)/2-t$ gives
  \begin{equation}
    S_4(Q_r)=\frac{t^2(4t^2-1)(4t^2+1)}{15}.
    \label{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-wrapped}
  \end{equation}
  For an explicit division certificate, let $E$ denote the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:gaussian-collision-moment} and let $F$ denote the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-wrapped}.
  Reduction by $i^2=-1$ gives the exact identity
  \begin{equation}
    60(E-F)=-(u+iv)H(u,v,t),
    \label{eq:gaussian-division-certificate}
  \end{equation}
  where
  \begin{align*}
    H={}&240t^4u-240it^4v+(-480+480i)t^3uv-60t^2u^3\\
       &+(240-180i)t^2u^2v+(180-240i)t^2uv^2+60it^2v^3\\
       &+(-120+120i)tu^2v^2+u^5-iu^4v+14u^3v^2\\
       &-14iu^2v^3+uv^4-u-iv^5+iv.
  \end{align*}
  Since $u+iv=0$ in $\Fq$, this proves
  \eqref{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-wrapped}.
  By~\eqref{eq:gaussian-wrapped-bound}, the three positive factors
  $t$, $4t^2-1$, and $4t^2+1$ are all strictly smaller than $q$.
  Hence the wrapped fourth moment is non-zero.

  In both cases, \eqref{eq:gaussian-fourth-moment-invariance} forces $a^4=1$.
  Thus $A_{Q_r(i)}\subseteq\langle i\rangle$, while the reverse inclusion follows from
  the rotational symmetry of the box.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:box-degenerate}
  When the $2r+1$ rows of $Q_r(i)$ are pairwise non-adjacent cyclic intervals, the box has
  $m=2r+1$ runs, each of length exactly $m$.
  The family therefore sits exactly on the boundary excluded by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}, as it must.
  For $r=1$ the proposition recovers the depth-zero square of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families}.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser} proves that every proper direct or wrapped
  Gaussian box has stabiliser exactly $C_4$.
\end{remark}

Near saturation, Gaussian boxes also occur in wrapped form: for $q<8r^2$ the reductions of
the $(2r+1)^2$ lattice points can collide, and the resulting set can cover all but a few
residues.
These wrapped boxes fall outside the hypothesis $q>8r^2$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-boxes}; they are not realisations of that proposition, but
their exact stabiliser is covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser}.
The audited adjacent realisation of Proposition~\ref{prop:near-saturation} at $q=73$,
with $K^{\mathrm{c}}=\{13,14,59,60\}$, is of this wrapped-box type at radius $r=4$.

The refined inventory was then audited exhaustively.
For every prime $13\leq q\leq151$, every normalised admissible parameter pair and every
pre-saturation depth were enumerated, and the resulting exceptional mode sets were
deduplicated as exact sets, removing parameter multiplicities.
There are exactly $75$ distinct exceptional sets: $62$ with $A_K\cong C_4$ and $13$ with
$A_K\cong C_6$.
They fall into three refined mechanisms:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item $52$ Gaussian boxes, with radii from one through seven: uniform realisations of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-boxes}, together with boxes realised at other depths
  through modular coincidences of the nine-centre geometry, in direct or wrapped form;
  all have exact stabiliser $C_4$ by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser};
  \item $10$ four-point complements, each one a free $C_4$ orbit with exact stabiliser
  given by Corollary~\ref{cor:free-orbit-complement}, and none of box type;
  \item $13$ depth-zero $C_6$ sets, namely the Eisenstein family of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families}.
\end{enumerate}

No audited exceptional set falls outside these mechanisms, and no stabiliser order outside
$\{4,6\}$ occurs in the audited range.
Independent exhaustive order-eight searches at $q=193$, $241$, and $257$ cover
$4{,}540{,}161$ pre-saturation cases and find no $C_8$ instance.
These are exact finite audits, not a completeness theorem.

The residual open problem is sharpened accordingly: prove or refute that the three refined
mechanisms exhaust the degenerate regime for arbitrary odd primes, and that no stabiliser
order eight or higher ever occurs.
The Gaussian and free-complement $C_4$ mechanisms are now proved uniformly.
For the Eisenstein $C_6$ mechanism, Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families} proves the
stabiliser value, but a uniform identification of its top autocorrelation level set remains
open beyond the exact audit.
The unit groups of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and the Eisenstein integers provide a structural route
toward completeness, but no theorem yet excludes a fourth degenerate mechanism.

\section{The Internal Mackey Carrier}
\label{sec:carrier}

Equation~\eqref{eq:weyl-covariance} makes the Weyl basis a permutation basis for the
$\G$-action by conjugation.
There are two phase-space orbits: the origin and the punctured plane.
The stabiliser of a non-zero vector is a unipotent subgroup $U$ of order $q$.

\begin{proposition}[Internal induced representation]
  \label{prop:internal-carrier}
  As a $\G$-module under conjugation,
  \begin{equation}
    \End(V_c)\cong\Id\oplus\Ind_U^{\G}(\Id).
    \label{eq:internal-induced}
  \end{equation}
  Splitting the punctured orbit by $v\mapsto-v$ gives
  \begin{equation}
    \End(V_c)\cong\Id\oplus\Dplus\oplus\Dminus,
    \qquad
    \dim\Dplus=\dim\Dminus=\frac{q^2-1}{2}.
    \label{eq:parity-decomposition}
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Map the delta function at $v\in\Fq^2$ to $W_c(v)$.
  Weyl orthogonality makes this a vector-space isomorphism
  $\C[\Fq^2]\to\End(V_c)$, and covariance makes it $\G$-equivariant.
  The origin gives the trivial summand.
  The punctured plane is $\G/U$, proving~\eqref{eq:internal-induced}.
  The central element $-I$ acts freely on the punctured plane, so its two eigenspaces have
  equal dimension and give~\eqref{eq:parity-decomposition}.
\end{proof}

This proposition resolves a canonicity question.
The induced carrier is not an external representation added to the finite model.
It is already present in the bosonic operator algebra $\End(V_c)$.

\section{Hecke Doublets and Canonical Multiplicity Algebras}
\label{sec:hecke}

Let $Z=\{\pm I\}$ and let $H=ZU$.
The even sector is $\Dplus\cong\Ind_H^{\G}(\Id)$, while the odd sector is induced from the
non-trivial character of $Z$.
Induction in stages through a Borel $B=TU$ decomposes these modules into principal-series
representations.

For a character $\theta$ of $T/Z$, the Weyl element identifies the principal series
$\mathrm{PS}(\theta)$ and $\mathrm{PS}(\theta^{-1})$.
When $\theta\ne\theta^{-1}$, the two occurrences therefore form a canonical multiplicity
space $\C^2$.
The diagonal torus and the Weyl intertwiner generate $M_2(\C)$ on that space.
This is the relevant finite Hecke algebra, not merely the group algebra of the exchange
involution~\cite{Iwahori1964}.

\begin{proposition}[Even-sector commutant]
  \label{prop:even-commutant}
  If $q\equiv1\pmod4$, then
  \begin{equation}
    \End_{\G}(\Dplus)
    \cong\C^4\oplus M_2(\C)^{(q-5)/4}.
    \label{eq:even-commutant-one}
  \end{equation}
  If $q\equiv3\pmod4$, then
  \begin{equation}
    \End_{\G}(\Dplus)
    \cong\C^2\oplus M_2(\C)^{(q-3)/4}.
    \label{eq:even-commutant-three}
  \end{equation}
  In both cases the commutant dimension is $q-1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Decompose $\Ind_H^{\G}(\Id)$ by the characters of $T/Z$.
  Generic characters occur in inverse pairs and contribute one $M_2(\C)$ block per pair.
  The self-inverse trivial and quadratic characters split according to their central sign.
  For $q\equiv1\pmod4$, both belong to the even sector and contribute four scalar
  constituents in total.
  For $q\equiv3\pmod4$, only the trivial branch contributes to the even sector, giving two
  scalar constituents.
  Counting the remaining inverse pairs gives~\eqref{eq:even-commutant-one} and
  \eqref{eq:even-commutant-three}.
\end{proof}

The original finite Weil module $V_c$ itself has only its two irreducible parity factors and
a two-dimensional scalar commutant~\cite{Korinman2013}.
The multiplicity-two structure therefore appears strictly at the operator stratum
$\End(V_c)$, consistently with the irreducibility of the two parity factors.

\section{Harper Orientation of the Doublets}
\label{sec:harper}

Define the Hermitian finite Harper operator~\cite{Harper1955}
\begin{equation}
  T_c=W_c(X)+W_c(-X)+W_c(Y)+W_c(-Y).
  \label{eq:harper}
\end{equation}
Its Weyl symbol is supported on two transverse Lagrangian lines.
The two same-line contributions are orthogonal, while the transverse contribution is a
finite Gauss sum.

Let $\theta$ be a generic even torus character and let
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\theta,c}
  =\sum_{x\in\Fq^*}\theta^{-1}(x)\psi_c(x).
  \label{eq:gauss-sum}
\end{equation}
The classical Gauss identity gives $|\tau_{\theta,c}|^2=q$; its general finite-field
setting is part of the Davenport--Hasse theory~\cite{DavenportHasse1935}.

\begin{proposition}[Universal Harper split]
  \label{prop:harper-split}
  On every generic multiplicity doublet of $\Dplus$, the reduced Harper density has equal
  diagonal entries and non-zero off-diagonal entry proportional to
  $\tau_{\theta,c}$.
  After division by its scalar part, its two eigenvalues are
  \begin{equation}
    1-\frac{1}{\sqrt q},
    \qquad
    1+\frac{1}{\sqrt q}.
    \label{eq:harper-split}
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Homogeneous projection onto the pair
  $\{\theta,\theta^{-1}\}$ makes the same-line terms orthogonal with equal norms.
  Additive orthogonality reduces the two transverse terms to
  $2(q-1)\tau_{\theta,c}/q$.
  The diagonal term is $2(q-1)$ in the same normalisation.
  Since $|\tau_{\theta,c}|=\sqrt q$, the ratio of the off-diagonal modulus to the diagonal
  term is $q^{-1/2}$, proving~\eqref{eq:harper-split}.
\end{proof}

The phase of $\tau_{\theta,c}$ fixes the intertwiner gauge in each factor.
After this canonical Gauss normalisation, the two eigenlines are the symmetric and
antisymmetric Weyl combinations.
The splitting scale tends to zero as $q\to\infty$; no continuum mass gap follows without a
separate scaling prescription.

\section{Boundary Residues and Factorwise Mixing}
\label{sec:boundary}

Let $P_K$ be the Fourier-coordinate projector onto $W_K$ and define
\begin{equation}
  E_K=-P_KT_c(1-P_K)T_cP_K.
  \label{eq:residue}
\end{equation}
This is a negative semidefinite Hermitian square.
In the Fourier basis, the clock part of $T_c$ commutes with $P_K$ and drops out of
$(1-P_K)T_cP_K$.
Only the shifts by $\pm c$ remain.

Define the unscaled flanked-hole set
\begin{equation}
  H_c(K)=\{h\notin K:h-c,h+c\in K\}.
  \label{eq:holes}
\end{equation}
The diagonal part of $E_K$ is the negative outgoing shift degree.
Its Weyl symbol is real, even, and supported on one Lagrangian line, so it is scalar in every
generic multiplicity factor.
The off-diagonal part joins the two endpoints around each $h\in H_c(K)$.

For a generic even character $\theta$, set
\begin{equation}
  C_\theta(K)=\sum_{h\in H_c(K)}\theta\!\left(\frac{h}{2c}\right)
  \label{eq:boundary-character-sum}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\theta,c}(K)
  =C_\theta(K)\overline{\tau_{\theta,c}}
  -\overline{C_\theta(K)}\tau_{\theta,c}.
  \label{eq:phase-determinant}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact factorwise mixing criterion]
  \label{thm:mixing}
  The traceless multiplicity densities of $T_c$ and $E_K$ generate $M_2(\C)$ in the generic
  $\theta$ factor if and only if
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\theta,c}(K)\ne0.
    \label{eq:mixing-criterion}
  \end{equation}
  If $H_c(K)=\varnothing$ or $C_\theta(K)=0$, then the second axis is absent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The boundary reduction above makes the off-diagonal component of the reduced $E_K$
  density proportional to $C_\theta(K)$.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:harper-split} makes the Harper off-diagonal component proportional
  to $\tau_{\theta,c}$.
  Two Hermitian $2\times2$ matrices generate $M_2(\C)$ exactly when their traceless
  three-component coefficient vectors are not collinear.
  Their oriented area is proportional to~\eqref{eq:phase-determinant}, giving the
  criterion.
\end{proof}

When~\eqref{eq:mixing-criterion} holds, orthogonalising the traceless part of $E_K$ against
that of $T_c$ and orienting the third axis by $i[T_c,E_K]$ gives a canonical ordered
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ frame.
This construction is conjugation-covariant and therefore independent of the phase convention
used to realise the principal-series factor.

Theorem~\ref{thm:spacing} decides when a boundary coefficient can occur.
The two remaining gates of~\eqref{eq:mixing-criterion} are both closed by the next results:
the phase-determinant test is redundant for every generic character, and the exact
vanishings of $C_\theta(K)$ are completely classified.

\begin{theorem}[Galois separation]
  \label{thm:galois-separation}
  Let $\theta$ have order greater than two.
  For every odd prime $q$, every non-zero central character $c$, and every mode set $K$ with
  $0\notin\HK$, so that every term of~\eqref{eq:boundary-character-sum} is defined,
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\theta,c}(K)\ne0
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    C_\theta(K)\ne0.
    \label{eq:galois-separation}
  \end{equation}
  The restriction is vacuous for the filtration sets: $0\in K_n$ by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set}, so $0\notin H_c(K_n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  If $C_\theta(K)=0$, then $\Delta_{\theta,c}(K)=0$ by~\eqref{eq:phase-determinant}.
  For the converse, note that $C_\theta(K)$ and $\overline{C_\theta(K)}$ lie in
  $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q-1})$, while $\tau_{\theta,c}\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q(q-1)})$.
  For $a\in\Fq^*$, let $\sigma_a$ be the automorphism of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q(q-1)})$ that
  fixes $\zeta_{q-1}$ and sends $\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^a$.
  Then $\sigma_a$ fixes $C_\theta(K)$ and $\overline{C_\theta(K)}$, while direct substitution
  in the Gauss sum gives
  $\sigma_a(\tau_{\theta,c})=\tau_{\theta,ac}=\theta(a)\,\tau_{\theta,c}$ and
  $\sigma_a(\overline{\tau_{\theta,c}})=\theta(a)^{-1}\overline{\tau_{\theta,c}}$.
  Suppose $\Delta_{\theta,c}(K)=0$ with $C_\theta(K)\ne0$.
  Since $|\tau_{\theta,c}|^2=q$, the common value
  $C_\theta(K)\overline{\tau_{\theta,c}}=\overline{C_\theta(K)}\,\tau_{\theta,c}$ is
  non-zero.
  Applying $\sigma_a$ and comparing with the original equality forces $\theta(a)^2=1$ for
  every $a\in\Fq^*$, contradicting $\mathrm{ord}(\theta)>2$.
\end{proof}

The vanishing locus of the boundary character sum is then controlled by a geometric bound on
the number of hole pairs.

\begin{lemma}[At most four hole pairs]
  \label{lem:hole-pairs}
  For every admissible block and pre-saturation depth $n$, the flanked-hole set satisfies
  $\HK=-\HK$ with $0\notin\HK$, every rescaled hole is the midpoint of an inter-centre arc
  of cyclic spacing exactly $2n+4$, and
  \begin{equation}
    r=\tfrac12\,|\HK|\leq4.
    \label{eq:hole-pair-bound}
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Rescale by $c^{-1}$ as in~\eqref{eq:centre-set}, so that $c^{-1}K_n$ is the union of the
  radius-$(n+1)$ windows centred on $S=-S$ with $0\in S$.
  By the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:spacing}, a flanked hole is a width-one uncovered
  component between two consecutive windows, so its site is the midpoint of the arc between
  two consecutive distinct centres of cyclic spacing exactly $2n+4$, and distinct arcs give
  distinct holes.
  Negation permutes the $|S|\leq9$ inter-centre arcs and reverses the cyclic order.
  An arc fixed by negation runs from some $s$ to $-s$; a hole at its midpoint would satisfy
  $h=-h$, hence $h=0$, which is impossible because $0\in S\subseteq c^{-1}K_n$.
  Therefore all porous arcs pair off freely under negation, holes come in pairs $\{h,-h\}$
  with $0\notin\HK$, and at most $\lfloor9/2\rfloor=4$ arc pairs can share the spacing value
  $2n+4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete classification of the boundary-sum zeros]
  \label{thm:zero-classification}
  Let $\theta$ be a generic even character of order $d>2$ and let $h_1,\ldots,h_r$ represent
  the pairs $\{h,-h\}$ of $\HK$, with $r\leq4$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:hole-pairs}.
  Then
  \begin{equation}
    C_\theta(K)=2\,\theta\bigl((2c)^{-1}\bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta(h_i),
    \label{eq:paired-boundary-sum}
  \end{equation}
  and $C_\theta(K)=0$ exactly in the following cases.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $r=2$ and $\theta(h_1/h_2)=-1$; this requires $d$ even.
    \item $r=3$ and $\theta(h_2/h_1)$, $\theta(h_3/h_1)$ are the two primitive cube roots of
    unity; this requires $3\mid d$.
    \item $r=4$ and the four values match into two antipodal pairs,
    $\theta(h_i/h_j)=\theta(h_k/h_l)=-1$ for a partition $\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,l\}$ of the
    indices; this requires $d$ even.
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular $C_\theta(K)\ne0$ whenever $r=1$, and no other vanishing mechanism occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Evenness of $\theta$ and $\HK=-\HK$ give~\eqref{eq:paired-boundary-sum}.
  Each listed relation forces the sum to vanish, so only necessity requires proof.
  Write $z_i=\theta(h_i)$, so that $C_\theta(K)=0$ if and only if $z_1+\cdots+z_r=0$ with
  every $z_i$ a $d$-th root of unity.
  For $r=1$ the sum is a root of unity and cannot vanish.
  For $r=2$, $z_1+z_2=0$ means $z_2=-z_1$, which is $\theta(h_1/h_2)=-1$.
  For $r=3$, divide by $z_1$ and set $\eta=z_2/z_1$, $\mu=z_3/z_1$, so that $1+\eta+\mu=0$.
  Then $\mu=-1-\eta$ and $|\mu|=1$ give $(1+\eta)(1+\bar\eta)=1$, hence
  $\eta+\bar\eta=-1$: the unit number $\eta$ has real part $-\tfrac12$, so it is a
  primitive cube root of unity and $\mu=\bar\eta$ is the other one.
  For $r=4$, suppose first that $s=z_1+z_2\ne0$.
  Conjugation inverts unit numbers, so
  $\bar s=z_1^{-1}+z_2^{-1}=s/(z_1z_2)$, hence $z_1z_2=s/\bar s$.
  The pair $\{-z_3,-z_4\}$ has sum $-(z_3+z_4)=s$ and product
  $z_3z_4=(-s)/(-\bar s)=s/\bar s$ by the same identity.
  Two pairs with equal sum and equal product are the root pairs of one quadratic, so
  $\{z_1,z_2\}=\{-z_3,-z_4\}$ and the four values match into two antipodal pairs.
  If instead $z_1+z_2=0$, then $z_3+z_4=0$ and the matching is immediate.
  The order conditions follow because $-1$ requires $2\mid d$ and a primitive cube root
  requires $3\mid d$.
\end{proof}

The case analysis above is a self-contained instance of the general theory of vanishing
sums of roots of unity: the possible lengths of vanishing sums of $d$-th roots of unity
form the numerical semigroup generated by the primes dividing $d$~\cite{LamLeung2000}, and
short vanishing sums were analysed in~\cite{ConwayJones1976}.

\begin{corollary}[Odd-order factors prime to three always mix]
  \label{cor:odd-order-mixing}
  If $\mathrm{ord}(\theta)>2$ is odd and not divisible by three, then $C_\theta(K)\ne0$ for
  every porous mode set, and by Theorems~\ref{thm:mixing} and~\ref{thm:galois-separation}
  every porous depth produces full $M_2(\C)$ mixing in the $\theta$ factor.
\end{corollary}

Every hole representative is a half-sum of two adjacent centres, so each ratio $h_i/h_j$ is
an explicit rational function of the normalised parameters $(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)$.
The complete mixing criterion is therefore a finite list of explicit character conditions on
the block.

\section{Asymptotic Distribution of Porous Depths}
\label{sec:porous-asymptotics}

Theorem~\ref{thm:spacing} makes porosity a spacing-parity event.
This section determines its exact asymptotic law in the natural uniform ensemble: the
normalised parameters $(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)$ are uniform on $\Fq^2$, with the loci of
vanishing constituents and of centre collisions removed.
Those loci lie on finitely many affine lines, hence contain $O(q)$ of the $q^2$ pairs and do
not affect the limit.

\begin{lemma}[Four spacing pairs]
  \label{lem:four-spacings}
  Suppose the nine centres of~\eqref{eq:centre-set} are distinct.
  Let $x_1<x_2<x_3<x_4$ be the sorted circular distances from zero of the four form values
  $\widetilde u$, $\widetilde v$, $\widetilde u+\widetilde v$, $\widetilde u-\widetilde v$,
  and set $d_1=x_1$ and $d_j=x_j-x_{j-1}$ for $j=2,3,4$.
  Then the cyclic spacings of the centre set are $d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4$, each occurring twice,
  together with $q-2x_4$, which is odd.
  Consequently, if additionally the $d_j$ are pairwise distinct, the porous depths of the
  block are exactly the values $n_j=(d_j-4)/2$ over the indices with $d_j$ even and
  $d_j\geq4$; in particular there are at most four porous depths.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The centre set is symmetric and contains zero, and $q$ is odd, so exactly one
  representative of each pair $\{w,-w\}$ lies in the open interval $(0,q/2)$.
  Reading the circle from zero, the positive-side centre positions are the $x_j$, the
  spacings on that side are $d_1,\ldots,d_4$, and central symmetry repeats them on the
  negative side.
  The remaining arc, from the position at distance $x_4$ to its mirror image, has length
  $q-2x_4$, which is odd because $q$ is odd.
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:spacing}, an even spacing $d_j\geq4$ yields flanked holes at depth
  $n_j=(d_j-4)/2$ and odd spacings yield none.
  At depth $n_j$ the holes themselves are uncovered sites, so $K_{n_j}$ is proper and the
  depth is pre-saturation.
\end{proof}

The parity of the circular distance of a form value is an explicit function of the parities
of $\widetilde u$ and $\widetilde v$ and of the nearest-multiple branch, because $q$ is odd.
This motivates the following limit object.

\begin{definition}[Limiting point process]
  \label{def:porous-limit}
  Let $(U,V)$ be Haar-uniform on $(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^2$ and let
  $\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v$ be independent uniform bits, independent of $(U,V)$.
  For a linear form $\alpha U+\beta V$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\{0,\pm1\}$, let
  $\|\alpha U+\beta V\|=|\alpha U+\beta V-k|$ be its distance to the nearest integer $k$,
  and define its parity as $\alpha\epsilon_u+\beta\epsilon_v+k\bmod2$.
  Let $z_1<z_2<z_3<z_4$ be the sorted values of
  $\|U\|,\|V\|,\|U+V\|,\|U-V\|$, with gaps $g_1=z_1$ and $g_j=z_j-z_{j-1}$.
  The point process $\Xi$ puts one atom at $g_1/2$ when the parity of the closest form is
  zero, and one atom at $g_j/2$ for $j\geq2$ when the parities of the two adjacent forms
  agree.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Point-process convergence]
  \label{thm:porous-convergence}
  Let $\Xi_q$ put one unit atom at $n/q$ for every porous depth $n$ of the uniform
  admissible block at the odd prime $q$.
  Then $\Xi_q\Rightarrow\Xi$ in distribution as $q\to\infty$ through odd primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The vector $(\widetilde u/q,\widetilde v/q,\widetilde u\bmod2,\widetilde v\bmod2)$
  converges in distribution to Haar measure times the uniform two-bit measure, by
  elementary Riemann sums; the removed degenerate loci have vanishing weight.
  For each form, the integer circular distance is $|\alpha\widetilde u+\beta\widetilde v-kq|$
  for the nearest multiple $kq$, and since $q$ is odd its parity is
  $\alpha\widetilde u+\beta\widetilde v+k\bmod2$, matching
  Definition~\ref{def:porous-limit} on the corresponding branch.
  The nearest-integer branches, the sorting of the four distances, and the parity selector
  of Lemma~\ref{lem:four-spacings} are locally constant or continuous away from finitely
  many affine walls, which have Haar measure zero.
  Finally, $n_j/q=d_j/(2q)-2/q$ and $d_j/(2q)\to g_j/2$, so the atom positions converge to
  those of $\Xi$.
  Hence every bounded continuous functional of the point configuration converges, proving
  the claim.
\end{proof}

The limit law is then computable in closed form.

\begin{theorem}[Exact limit law]
  \label{thm:porous-law}
  Let $N=\Xi([0,1/4])$ be the limiting number of porous depths.
  Then
  \begin{equation}
    \Pr(N=0,1,2,3,4)
    =\left(\tfrac{3}{20},\ \tfrac{7}{24},\ \tfrac{9}{40},\ \tfrac{5}{24},\ \tfrac18\right),
    \label{eq:porous-count-law}
  \end{equation}
  so that $\Pr(N\geq1)=\tfrac{17}{20}$ and $\mathbb{E}[N]=\tfrac{28}{15}$.
  The intensity measure of $\Xi$ is absolutely continuous on $[0,1/4]$ with piecewise affine
  density
  \begin{equation}
    f(y)=
    \begin{cases}
      28-224y, & 0\leq y\leq\tfrac1{10},\\[0.2em]
      \tfrac{62}{3}-\tfrac{452}{3}\,y, & \tfrac1{10}\leq y\leq\tfrac18,\\[0.2em]
      \tfrac{14}{3}-\tfrac{68}{3}\,y, & \tfrac18\leq y\leq\tfrac16,\\[0.2em]
      \tfrac83-\tfrac{32}{3}\,y, & \tfrac16\leq y\leq\tfrac14,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:porous-intensity}
  \end{equation}
  of total mass $\tfrac{28}{15}$.
  Conditional on selecting one porous depth from the intensity, the mean normalised depth is
  $\tfrac{491}{10080}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  All quantities are finite-dimensional functionals of
  $(U,V,\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v)$.
  Partition $[0,1)^2$ by the nearest-integer branches of the four linear forms and by the
  $24$ possible orderings of the four distances.
  The resulting cells are convex rational polygons, and triangulating them yields $240$
  non-degenerate triangles.
  On each triangle every gap $g_j$ is an affine function of $(U,V)$, so its pushforward has
  a linear density supported on a rational interval.
  The parity weight of each atom is $\tfrac12$, except when the two adjacent forms are
  $U+V$ and $U-V$: their coefficient vectors coincide modulo two, so the parity agreement is
  determined by the branch indices rather than by the bits.
  Summing the $240$ exact contributions in rational arithmetic gives both the joint count
  law~\eqref{eq:porous-count-law} and the density~\eqref{eq:porous-intensity}; the
  computation is exact and machine-certified, in the sense of level~(iii) of
  Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, by the archived script
  \texttt{scripts/porous\_asymptotics.py} of the public paper
  repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Cosmochrony/o33}}, which reruns in under one
  second using only the standard library.
  Consistency checks close exactly: the five probabilities sum to one, the density is
  continuous with $f(1/4)=0$, its total mass equals
  $\mathbb{E}[N]=\tfrac{28}{15}$, and the first moment of $(15/28)f$ is
  $\tfrac{491}{10080}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:porous-finite-checks}
  Exact finite enumeration gives mean porous-depth counts $1.8584\ldots$ at $q=4001$ and
  $1.8625\ldots$ at $q=8009$, converging to $28/15=1.8666\ldots$ at the boundary rate
  expected from the affine walls.
  The descriptive seeded-sample fraction of non-porous blocks reported in
  Section~\ref{sec:audit} now has a proved limit: $\Pr(N=0)=3/20$.
\end{remark}

\section{Arithmetic Exceptions and Computational Certification}
\label{sec:audit}

The exact formulas were audited in three layers.
First, direct BFS enumeration at $q=29$ agrees with
Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set} for three deposited blocks and depths zero through two.
All eight previously deposited cases reproduce their exact dimensions and porous/smooth
dichotomy.

Second, a seeded sweep used 39 primes from 29 through 211 together with 307, with 30
admissible character blocks per prime.
It contains 1,170 blocks and 19,153 pre-saturation depth cases.
The spacing criterion has no failure.
An exhaustive audit over all normalised admissible parameter pairs at
$q=13,17,29,31,37,41,43,53$ also has no spacing failure.
These checks guard the proof but do not replace it.

The multiplicative stabiliser is $\{\pm1\}$ in the eight deposited cases.
The random sweep contains seven order-four exceptions.
An exhaustive audit of every normalised admissible pair and every pre-saturation depth for
all primes $13\leq q\leq73$ finds only orders two, four, and six.
The order-four cases occur for
$q=13,17,29,37,41,53,61,73$, while the order-six cases occur for
$q=31,37,43,61,67,73$.
A separate exact seeded audit through $q=499$ tested 12,234 cases surviving the divisor
sieve and found no order beyond six.
Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptional-families} was also checked for every applicable prime
through $997$.
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}, these finite audits reduce the conjecture that
every proper mode set of the present form has $A_K\cong C_2$, $C_4$, or $C_6$ to the
degenerate regime of Corollary~\ref{cor:degenerate-reduction}, where it remains open.

The degenerate regime itself was then audited exhaustively as described in
Section~\ref{sec:degenerate}.
For every prime $13\leq q\leq151$, all normalised admissible pairs and pre-saturation
depths were enumerated and the exceptional mode sets deduplicated as exact sets.
The audit finds exactly $75$ distinct exceptional sets, $62$ of order four and $13$ of
order six, classified without remainder into $52$ Gaussian boxes, $10$ non-box four-point
complement orbits, and $13$ depth-zero Eisenstein sets.
The uniform realisations of Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-boxes} were verified exactly
for every applicable prime in the audited range.
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser} now proves the exact $C_4$ stabiliser of every
proper box, including the wrapped near-saturation boxes outside the hypothesis
$q>8r^2$ of that proposition.
Corollary~\ref{cor:free-orbit-complement} proves the exact $C_4$ stabiliser of every free
four-point complement orbit.
Separate exhaustive order-eight searches at $q=193$, $241$, and $257$ enumerate
$4{,}540{,}161$ pre-saturation cases and find no $C_8$ instance.
The completeness kill-switch pre-registered before this audit had two branches: an
exceptional order outside $\{2,4,6\}$, or a mechanism not covered by the three published
family statements.
The second branch fired: the Gaussian-box family of Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-boxes}
is exactly such a mechanism, and the exhaustion reading is withdrawn in
Section~\ref{sec:degenerate}.
The residual kill-switch, reformulated after the refinement --- an exceptional order
outside $\{2,4,6\}$ or an audited set outside the three refined mechanisms --- did not
fire anywhere in the audited range.

The rigidity chain of Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} was validated by the same exact machinery.
An exhaustive sweep over all normalised centre parameters and pre-saturation depths for
$q\leq73$ (256,229 cases) and a seeded sweep of sixty blocks per prime for
$74\leq q\leq250$ (42,878 cases) checked 599,020 instances of
Lemma~\ref{lem:almost-period}, 408,558 residue bounds of
Lemma~\ref{lem:run-correspondence}, and 204,279 instances of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity},
with no failure.
All 355 exceptional cases in these sweeps (307 of order four, 48 of order six) have a run
or a gap of length at most $m$, as Corollary~\ref{cor:degenerate-reduction} requires.

Third, Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-classification} was verified adversarially by two independent
exact computations on every instance: a zero test by remainder division of the exponent
polynomial by the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_d$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, and a combinatorial
decomposition of the exponent multiset into antipodal pairs and cube triples.
This audit is exhaustive over every normalised admissible block and every pre-saturation
depth for all odd primes $7\leq q\leq73$, extended by a seeded sweep of sixty blocks per
prime for $79\leq q\leq311$.
It contains 31,166 porous cases and 1,069,192 exact zero tests, with no mismatch between the
two verdicts.
The hole-pair counts are $r=1$ in 21,828 cases, $r=2$ in 6,011, $r=3$ in 3,163, and $r=4$
in 164, so the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:hole-pairs} is attained.
The zeros split into 3,620 antipodal-pair factors, 1,030 cube-triple factors, and six
double-pair factors.
All six double-pair zeros occur for characters of order exactly four, with the four hole
representatives in arithmetic progression, at $q=97$ (two blocks) and $q=137$ (one block).
The 1,186 porous cases of the earlier seeded sweep are reproduced exactly: 50 cases carry a
zero, in 168 zero character indices (84 inverse-paired generic factors), classified as 120
antipodal-pair and 48 cube-triple instances.
Two pre-registered kill-switches, namely a case with $r\geq5$ and an exact zero outside the
classified decompositions, did not fire anywhere in the audited range.
No numerical phase scan is required: Theorem~\ref{thm:galois-separation} settles the phase
determinant uniformly.

In the random sample, 294 of 1,170 blocks have no porous pre-saturation depth.
The fraction $25\%$ is descriptive of the seeded sample at small primes.
Its asymptotic value is now a theorem: by Theorem~\ref{thm:porous-law} the limiting
non-porous probability is $3/20$, and exact enumerations at $q=4001$ and $q=8009$ show the
finite mean porous count converging to $28/15$ as stated in
Remark~\ref{rem:porous-finite-checks}.

\section{Parity Protection and Calibrated Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Let $R$ be Schr\"odinger parity, $(Rf)(x)=f(-x)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
  RW_c(v)R^{-1}=W_c(-v).
  \label{eq:parity-weyl}
\end{equation}
The $R$-even and $R$-odd operator spaces are respectively
$\Id\oplus\Dplus$ and $\Dminus$.

\begin{theorem}[Parity-closure no-go]
  \label{thm:parity-closure}
  The deposited operators $P_{K_n}$, $T_c$, $(1-P_{K_n})T_cP_{K_n}$, and $E_{K_n}$ are
  $R$-even.
  The unital star-algebra they generate, including ordered compositions, functional calculus,
  and norm limits, has zero projection onto $\Dminus$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:closed-mode-set} gives $K_n=-K_n$, hence $P_{K_n}$ is even.
  The four terms in~\eqref{eq:harper} occur in opposite pairs, hence $T_c$ is even.
  Products and sums of even operators are even because
  $R(AB)R^{-1}=(RAR^{-1})(RBR^{-1})$.
  Adjoints, functional calculus, and norm limits preserve the same identity.
  The odd projection therefore vanishes throughout the generated algebra.
\end{proof}

For $q\equiv1\pmod4$, the odd-sector commutant is
\begin{equation}
  \End_{\G}(\Dminus)\cong M_2(\C)^{(q-1)/4}.
  \label{eq:odd-commutant}
\end{equation}
For $q\equiv3\pmod4$, the quadratic character moves to the odd sector and contributes its
two scalar constituents, giving
\begin{equation}
  \End_{\G}(\Dminus)\cong\C^2\oplus M_2(\C)^{(q-3)/4}.
  \label{eq:odd-commutant-three}
\end{equation}
Thus the protected sector is not empty.
It is a pure family of multiplicity doublets when $q\equiv1\pmod4$, while the
$q\equiv3\pmod4$ branch also contains two scalar constituents.
An algebraically natural odd operator would be
\begin{equation}
  T_c^{\mathrm{odd}}
  =i\bigl(W_c(X)-W_c(-X)+W_c(Y)-W_c(-Y)\bigr).
  \label{eq:odd-harper}
\end{equation}
It requires a choice inside each generator pair.
The directed Q11 frontier explicitly selects no subset of generators, and its arrow is
radial in BFS depth~\cite{Beau2026q11of}.
Projective temporal ordering orders that same radial depth variable~\cite{Beau2026pto}.
Neither construction presently supplies the phase-space-odd lift
\eqref{eq:odd-harper}.

\subsection*{Interpretive outlook}

The proved mathematical structure is a parity-graded operator architecture.
The even sector carries a universal finite split and, when the boundary character sum is
non-zero, a second axis and a complete two-level frame.
The odd sector carries further doublets but is protected from all operators in the deposited
even algebra.

It is structurally tempting to compare the first sector with bosonic symmetry breaking and
the protected sector with a missing chiral datum.
That comparison is interpretive only.
The construction does not identify electroweak charges, does not transport the doublets to a
fermionic matter carrier, and does not produce an unsquared left--right mass operator.
The residue $E_K$ remains of $-M^*M$ type.

The exact advance toward masses is consequently limited but concrete: the theory derives
two-level mass-square kinematics, a finite split of size $q^{-1/2}$, and arithmetic mixing
frames indexed by the filtration.
An absolute scale, a continuum law, and a chiral descent remain open.

The stabiliser problem is now localised and mechanically described.
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} proves $A_K=\{\pm1\}$ whenever every run and gap of the mode set
is longer than the run count, so exceptional orders occur only in the degenerate boundary
regime of Corollary~\ref{cor:degenerate-reduction}.
That regime hosts the uniform Gaussian-box $C_4$ family of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gaussian-boxes}, which contains the depth-zero square, together with
wrapped boxes near saturation, non-box four-point complement orbits, and the depth-zero
Eisenstein $C_6$ family.
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-box-stabiliser} proves the exact stabiliser of every proper
Gaussian box, and Corollary~\ref{cor:free-orbit-complement} proves it for every free
four-point complement orbit.
The exhaustive audit through $q=151$ and the empty order-eight searches at
$q=193$, $241$, and $257$ support, without proving, the completeness of these three refined
mechanisms; the earlier exhaustion reading of the family statements is withdrawn.
The remaining completeness question is to prove or refute that the refined mechanisms and
the orders four and six exhaust the degenerate regime for arbitrary odd primes.
The top autocorrelation level of the Eisenstein $C_6$ sets remains audit-certified rather
than uniformly proved.
The unit groups of the Gaussian and Eisenstein integers provide an organising principle
for this residual question, not a completeness theorem.
The mixing question is closed: Theorem~\ref{thm:galois-separation} removes the phase
determinant for every generic character, and Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-classification} lists
the only three vanishing mechanisms of the boundary character sum, on at most four hole
pairs.
The porous-frequency question is also closed: Theorems~\ref{thm:porous-convergence}
and~\ref{thm:porous-law} identify the limiting point process of porous depths, its rational
count law with mean $28/15$, and its piecewise affine intensity.
